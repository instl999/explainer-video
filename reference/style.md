# Look, story and pacing

## What the user has told us (do not repeat these mistakes)
- **"Came across more like a promotional video"** — a cut with gold serif titles, glass cards, light leaks, light-ray
  title cards and inspirational 结尾升华 music was rejected. Explain a mechanism instead of glamorising it.
- **"The first 20 seconds must grab viewers, otherwise the video is a total failure, however good the rest is."**
- **"Code-generated music and effects were the weakest part"** — use only their libraries, picked automatically.
- They want the copy and footage to be a starting point, not a cage ("别让现有的文案和视频限制住你的思路"), except
  when they say to keep the opening hook — then keep it verbatim.

## The look
- **Paper boards** for the explaining: warm paper `#EEEAE1` with a dot grid, ink `#1A1A1A`, hand-drawn lines
  (`inkPath`, `ring`, `strike`, `arrow`), cards with soft shadows, rubber `stamp`s, a yellow `marker` sweep.
- **Annotated footage** for the story: white/red callouts with leader lines, rings around the thing being named,
  pills, kinetic white type over a dark shade (`leftShade`/`rightShade`/`topShade`) or on a dark `plate`.
- **Colour has meaning:** one red (`C.red`) for loss/danger/alarm/debt, one blue (`C.blue`) for money kept, calm,
  choice, needs; yellow (`C.marker`) for the word that matters; everything else ink greys.
- **Type:** Display = 得意黑 SmileySans (titles, kinetic words), Sans = Noto Sans SC Bold (body, labels), Num =
  Bahnschrift (digits; mixed in automatically), Mono/MonoB = Consolas, LCD = DSEG7, JP = Yu Gothic (Japanese).
- Every frame: a background (footage or paper), a subject (diagram, hero element), and something moving.
- Label stand-in footage ("示意画面") when a person on screen is not who the voice names; show footnotes with sources
  and assumptions in small grey (`foot`) and keep them off the caption band.

## The hook (first ~20 s)
1. Frame 0: an impact (`impacts` flash 0.5–0.6 + punch), a boom (`hitOpen`), voice by 0.35 s.
2. Line 1 is the most surprising concrete thing: a counter-intuitive study result, a calculated number the viewer feels
   ("每天8小时，有4个多小时在替银行打工"), or a dare ("不信，现在就去打开你家冷冻室"). The user's own hook line if
   they want it kept.
3. Prove it at once (the experiment, the worked sum, the photo wall), then turn to the viewer ("你是不是也一样？").
4. Hard cuts every 1.5–2.5 s, a kinetic word or stamp on each key word; music accents on story beats; a tape-stop and a
   beat of silence under a twist line is strong.
5. Background (who the author is, context) comes after the hook, briefly.

## Structure that has worked (60–75 s)
hook (claim → proof → "是不是你") → mechanism (draw the cause as a picture: a loop, a bar draining, a receipt
printing, bins sorting) → the turn (a different bed, brighter footage) → the fix as 1–2 concrete steps → the user's CTA
(comment keyword, follow) → a short warm end title.

## Pacing rules
- Something new on screen every ~1 s; a cut or camera move every 2–4 s after the hook.
- Land each new element on the word that names it (`say`, `popS(t, A(...))`); hits/stamps just **after** the keyword.
- A payoff needs 0.4–0.8 s on screen before the cut: add `pause` after the line or a `hold` inside it (both free).
- Never leave a board empty for more than ~0.5 s: bring the main object in with the shot, reveal details on the words.
- Text never sits on the bottom caption band (y > ~900) and never overlaps the subject's face.

## Writing and facts
- One sentence per line, 8–30 characters; spoken Chinese, not written; no filler.
- Numbers on screen are verified or derived on screen ("200,000 ÷ 8,000 = 25 个月"); examples say they are examples.
- Cite on screen in small type (paper/authors/year); add a critique note when a study is disputed.
- Attribute quotes ("她在书里写：…", with the original language when it adds credibility).
- No medical/investment advice; the CTA is the user's.
